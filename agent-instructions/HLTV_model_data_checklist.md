# HLTV forecasting data collection checklist

**Start with one row per completed series, containing both team IDs, the result, and when that result became available.** That archive supports series Bradley–Terry, team Glicko-2, and an Elo baseline. Add actual player IDs for player TrueSkill, played maps for hierarchical map Bradley–Terry, and historical observations with their denominators for the GAM. All four can share the same archive.

This checklist uses the saved GamerLegion–Luminosity result as its worked example. The [match-page field guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_d9bde9b96f5081918c9ad58a3d55cd63&account_id=personal) gives exact selectors. Source descriptions below distinguish fields found in that HTML from additional records you would need to obtain. Detailed stats pages and demos are candidate sources whose contents still need verification.

## 1. Collect once for all four models

| Record and grain | Raw fields to save | Where to get them |
|---|---|---|
| Series · one per public match ID | Public match ID and URL, separate series stats ID and URL, event ID, both ordered team IDs and names, scheduled start UTC, status, final score in maps, winner ID, raw match notes. Preserve forfeit, walkover, cancellation, awarded-map or incomplete status when documented. | Canonical URL, `.teamsBox`, `.maps .preformatted-text`, and the detailed-stats link. Keep uncertain result types explicit. |
| Timing · one per observation or change | Source URL, capture time UTC, published/effective time if known, precision and evidence. For results, save actual completion time if documented and earliest time known final. For each forecast, save its cutoff and whether it is pre-veto or post-veto. | Start time is on the page. A saved “Match over” page proves the result was available by capture time. It gives no precise finish time. |
| Context · one per match, with dated event reference | Best-of, LAN/online, stage text, bracket, round, event name and ID, displayed team regions. Add competition region, event tier, tier source, venue and city if independently documented. | Visible format block, header and event link. Here the format block says LAN, while JSON-LD says “Online Event.” A team’s Europe flag does not locate the event. |
| Team identity · one per dated identity or affiliation | HLTV team ID, name as displayed, alias, organization ID if known, relevant roster/core ID, effective dates, evidence, and change type such as rename, transfer of core, new roster or unresolved. | Historical result pages establish team names and lineups at appearances. Use dated roster or organization records to establish continuity across different IDs. Keep original IDs when identities are disputed. |
| Coverage · one per event or collection interval | Event/date range searched, expected result IDs when available, fetched IDs, missing or partial pages, and which sections were available. | Use event result listings or a dated results index to discover matches. The three-month opponent box and head-to-head list supply additional links. |

**Historical backfill.** Follow the links to individual historical results and ingest each public match ID once. Collect earlier matches for opponents of the teams you follow, including qualifiers and cross-region events. Rows in the Team and Core tabs can overlap, and both teams can link to the same result. The card “3 weeks ago” is a discovery aid. Open the linked result for its scheduled timestamp, final score and actual players. Keep a missing page distinguishable from a team having played no matches.

**Time rule for all inputs.** Historical scores and player performance become eligible for a forecast only when the underlying result was available by its cutoff. Preserve timestamped evidence for target-match context that can change, especially lineups and vetoes. Where an old capture cannot establish ordering within a day or rating period, flag that ambiguity. Do not use the scheduled start as the completion time.

## 2. Bradley–Terry

**Minimum dataset:** the series archive above. One observation must identify *who beat whom*. A team's season win–loss total discards the opponent information.

- [ ] **Schedule and opponent history.** Keep both team IDs on every series, even for unfamiliar opponents. Include older seasons, weaker teams and teams that later disbanded where coverage permits. Retain dates and event IDs for first appearances, long gaps and repeat meetings.
- [ ] **Schedule connections.** Record each team's dated competition region or tier when sourced, and collect qualifiers, international LANs and events where teams from different circuits meet. Keep events confined to one region too. These records reveal which schedules connect and where comparisons have little support.
- [ ] **Roster continuity.** Collect the five player IDs at each historical appearance, plus dated roster changes where documented. Preserve separately the date a change was announced, its effective date and the first match in which the new five were observed.
- [ ] **Context extensions.** Save LAN/online, series format, stage, event tier and team regions. For a map forecast, also save the map, picker/decider, veto order and when that information was known. Preserve both team IDs within each context so team-specific effects can be studied.
- [ ] **Recent form, schedule strength and inactivity.** The raw inputs are the dated series, opponent IDs, earlier opponent results and coverage history. Collect the older results behind those opponents as well as the recent matches. There is no separate Bradley–Terry strength or strength-of-schedule field to scrape.

## 3. Team Glicko-2 and player TrueSkill

These need different levels of identity. Glicko-2 can start as soon as you have the dated team results. Player TrueSkill also needs to know who represented each team in each result and who could reasonably have been expected to play at forecast time.

| Collection item | Team Glicko-2 | Player TrueSkill |
|---|---|---|
| Historical outcomes | Both team IDs, outcome, match ID, reliable timing for assignment to a fixed rating period, including older matches before evaluation starts. | Same outcomes plus player IDs associated with each competing lineup. For a map experiment, use the map outcome and participants for that map. |
| Inactivity and missing coverage | Retain dates across gaps, event coverage, result availability and any borderline period assignments. Record the rating calendar’s time zone and period boundaries as dataset metadata. | Preserve appearance dates across all known teams for each player, including time with earlier organizations. |
| Actual participation | Team identity history covers team changes. Player data is optional for the team-only baseline. | For each team and map, save players listed in the map’s stats table. Keep series roster, map participants, starters, substitutes, and rounds played as distinct fields wherever documented. Mark partial participation or unknown exposure. |
| Expected lineup | Needed only for roster extensions. | Store announced or expected five, team and player IDs, source, announcement time, capture time, status and unknown slots. Keep subsequent replacements as later observations. |
| Transfers and stand-ins | Useful for documenting major breaks in a team's history. | Save prior team, new team, announcement/effective dates, stand-in or transfer label, source, and observed appearances. Retain repeated five-player combinations and appearances apart for players who move. |

- [ ] From the basic page, use lineup player IDs for the displayed five and player IDs in **each map's** table for actual listed participants. If the five change between maps, retain both lineups. A result-page lineup alone cannot establish the announced starting five, substitution time or formal stand-in status.
- [ ] Capture evidence for the same series population when comparing Glicko-2 with Elo. For player experiments, retain stable rosters, transfers and stand-ins in the archive, including uncertain classifications. Rating, rating deviation, volatility and player skill uncertainty are downstream model outputs.

## 4. Hierarchical Bayesian Bradley–Terry

**Minimum dataset:** a played-map table with match ID, event ID, map name, both team IDs and map winner. Preserve map order and series membership even if your first model uses only team and map.

- [ ] **Played maps and outcomes.** Save public match ID, map stats ID where present, map ordinal, map name, teams, final rounds, winner, and played/awarded/unplayed status. A planned decider may never be played. Keep a map with missing stats if its played result is documented.
- [ ] **Map and rules era.** Collect dated competitive map-pool membership, event-specific eligible maps, patch/build or tournament ruleset when documented, and effective dates. Unknown patch stays unknown. A patch release date alone does not establish when a tournament adopted it.
- [ ] **Veto and side.** Keep every veto step with actor, action, map and order. Distinguish a picked map from a leftover decider. Capture a published starting-side choice if available. The first half’s T/CT labels establish the observed starting side after play. Save segment order, each team’s side and rounds won by each team in every segment. Keep unallocated OT scores when side tags are absent.
- [ ] **Roster and roles.** For player or core effects, retain dated map-level participants and earlier appearances of each lineup. For roles, save player, team, map, side, role string, source, effective dates and observed exposure. The basic page identifies selected IGL/AWPer badges. CT positions, T positions and site assignments require additional evidence such as annotated demos. Keep lineup formation date distinct from the first match you collected.
- [ ] **Coverage for optional effects.** Collect the same team on multiple maps, the same map across teams, repeat matchups across events, and teams appearing at LAN and online. Keep team region, event region, tier, format and date. Preserve the intervening matches and roster changes. Those records establish which combinations actually repeat.
- [ ] **Within-series data.** Keep all maps from the same series under one match ID, in order, including defeat on an opponent’s pick, deciders and overtime. Also retain the final series outcome. This supports later comparisons of map and series predictions.

## 5. Binomial GAM

**Minimum dataset:** the shared series outcome archive and the raw histories behind the first predictors you choose. Only collect detailed rounds or demos for feature families you intend to test. The middle column below is the collection contract.

| Candidate feature family | Raw observations and denominators to save | Source and availability |
|---|---|---|
| Team strength, uncertainty, opponent-adjusted form | Prior series IDs, opponents, outcomes, dates, history coverage and team identity changes. For standings, save system, ranking date, team ID, rank, points if present, and publication/observation time. | Series archive; dated lineup badges for HLTV/VRS rank; VRS “before” box for its points. Elo/Glicko histories come from earlier results. |
| Win and round trends, context splits | Completed series and maps, ordered teams, scores in their original units, event, format, stage, LAN/online, map name, dates and counts of eligible series/maps/rounds. | Header and map cards across historical fixtures. A 2–1 series and a 13–7 map belong at different levels. |
| Map competence, breadth, picks and bans | Map outcomes, dated eligible pool, complete ordered veto, actor, maps still available at each decision or enough information to reconstruct them, pick/ban/decider, and missing-veto flag. | Map cards and veto blocks plus event pool/rules. Pre-veto forecasts use historical vetoes. A target-map pick belongs in a known-map forecast only after its publication. |
| Player form and lineup profile | Per historical player, team, map, side and date: kills, deaths, ADR, KAST, Swing, rating and rating version, separately marked eco-adjusted values, plus rounds actually played. Save damage totals and rounds satisfying KAST when available for exact aggregation. Retain each player’s row for lineup mean, weakest-player form, dispersion and star reliance. | Basic player tables have displayed metrics. Exact counts behind rounded percentages and individual exposure during substitutions need another source. Keep estimate precision. |
| Continuity, shared maps, stand-in experience and role-adjusted form | Dated lineup appearances, actual players per map, expected five at cutoff, member changes, prior appearances of each stand-in with these teammates, raw role and role exposure. | Historical player tables plus dated roster/announcement records. Keep per-player history after a transfer. |
| Schedule load, rest and travel | Prior match starts and verified finishes, played-map dates/order, event schedule, UTC and local event time zone. For travel, collect consecutive confirmed physical locations and dates, and travel timing if documented. | Start time from header; finish times, venues and itineraries need other records. An online match or team flag cannot establish where a player traveled. |
| Head-to-head | Both team IDs, historical match ID, map ID/name, players, event, date and scored outcome. Retain all eligible meetings and their units. | Use H2H links to retrieve original results. Preserve map count and distinct series count. |
| Side strength and pistol conversion | Every round’s number, regulation/OT segment, sides, winner, pistol-round indicator and next-round outcome when measuring follow-up conversion. Keep rounds played by side and eligible pistol or follow-up opportunities. | Regulation halves are on the basic page. Exact OT side attribution, pistol outcomes and sequences require verified round history. |
| Openings, trades and clutches | Opening duel participants and winner; kill, death and trade events with timestamps and player/team IDs; clutch attempts with initial 1vN state, participants and outcome. Keep eligible rounds, duel attempts, trade opportunities or clutch attempts according to the intended metric. | Inspect detailed stats or demos. The ten-player table has no dedicated columns for these counts. Record the source definition of a trade or clutch. |
| Economy, sites and retakes | Round ID, sides, opening equipment values or buy category for both teams, economy observation time, winner; bomb plant site, live players at plant, retake attempt and outcome. Keep eligible rounds, plants and retake opportunities separately. | Detailed round sources or parsed demos, subject to coverage. Eco-adjusted ADR alone cannot establish a team’s conversion record by buy type. |

For the proposed interactions, collect **both ingredients on the same dated observation**: team result history and roster changes; map history and the known picker; prior completion times and match volume. Store raw fields for both teams in a consistent orientation. Preserve the number of observed opportunities beside every rate, especially player–map–side and stand-in splits. Zero successes in ten opportunities and zero recorded opportunities are different observations.

## 6. First scrape and acceptance check

1. **Backfill series results first.** Save historical result URLs, outcomes, IDs and chronology. Start coverage before your intended evaluation window. This gives the first two team models a usable common dataset.
2. **Save cheap page sections on the same visit.** Keep maps, halves, vetoes, dated ranks, displayed lineups and every available player map/side row. Preserve the original page or capture reference so fields can be rechecked.
3. **Start pre-match captures while backfilling.** Save lineup, veto and schedule snapshots with UTC observation times. Historical results can be revisited later; the timing of an unarchived lineup announcement may never be recoverable.
4. **Add external sources by feature family.** First resolve identity and roster changes, event context and map-pool eras. Then verify specific round or demo fields needed by any advanced GAM or role experiment.
